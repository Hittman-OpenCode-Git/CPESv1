    D: The CAE reports functionally to the audit committee of the board of directors and administratively to the chief executive officer
  EC: IIA Standard 1110 (Organizational Independence) requires that the chief audit executive report to a level within the organization that allows the internal audit activity to fulfill its responsibilities. The CAE must confirm organizational independence to the board at least annually. IIA Standard 1110.A1 further specifies that the internal audit activity must be free from interference in determining the scope of internal auditing, performing work, and communicating results. The most effective reporting structure for supporting independence is dual reporting: functional reporting to the board (specifically the audit committee) for strategic direction, authority, and independence, and administrative reporting to the CEO for day-to-day operations, budgeting, and human resources. IIA Standard 1130 (Impairment to Independence or Objectivity) addresses situations where independence or objectivity is impaired in fact or appearance — if the CAE reports to the CFO (Choice A), independence is potentially impaired because internal audit is reviewing financial reporting processes that the CFO is responsible for. The CMA exam tests the principle that internal audit should be positioned high enough in the organization to be objective about the functions it audits.
  EW-A: Reporting to the CFO creates an independence impairment (IIA Standard 1130). Internal audit must be able to independently evaluate the financial reporting processes, controls, and disclosures for which the CFO is responsible. If the CAE reports to the CFO, internal audit is effectively auditing its own boss — a conflict of interest that undermines objectivity in both fact and appearance. This is the most common incorrect organizational placement and the one the CMA exam most frequently flags as a governance weakness.
  EW-B: Reporting to the general counsel similarly creates a reporting line within a function that internal audit may need to evaluate. Legal and compliance functions are subject to audit, and the CAE should not report to managers whose areas are within the audit scope. While coordination with legal and compliance is important, organizational independence requires that the CAE not be organizationally subordinate to the head of a function under audit.
  EW-C: Reporting to the COO subordinates internal audit to a member of management responsible for operations — the very activities that internal audit evaluates. Operational improvement recommendations are a valuable output of internal audit work, but the independence of those recommendations depends on the CAE not being beholden to the COO for performance evaluations, compensation, and career advancement. The dual-reporting model to the board (functional) and CEO (administrative) preserves independence while providing operational resources.
