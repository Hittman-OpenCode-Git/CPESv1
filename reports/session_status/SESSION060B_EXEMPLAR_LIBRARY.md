## 1. Stem Construction Template — Analyze/Evaluate Scenario

```
[Named Company], a [industry descriptor with size context],
[faces specific business problem with quantified stakes].
[Named Stakeholder], [with specific title/role],
[took specific action / proposes specific recommendation].
[Evidence data point 1 — dollar amount, percentage, count].
[Evidence data point 2 — time period showing pattern].
[Evidence data point 3 — competing stakeholder's position or audit finding].
[Evidence data point 4 — relevant framework threshold or boundary].
Under [COSO Principle N / COSO ERM / Three Lines model],
which [best evaluates / best supports / best characterizes / is most appropriate]?
```

### Minimum Requirements:
- 150+ words
- 4+ quantified data points
- At least 1 named stakeholder with title
- Decision tension (competing alternatives or positions)
- Framework reference (explicit COSO Principle or ERM concept)

### Avoid:
- Definition matching ("What is X called?")
- Vocabulary testing ("Which term means Y?")
- Pure recall stems
- Company-name-only rotation (same skeleton, different name)

## 2. ExplanationCorrect — Five-Part Mandatory Structure

```
PART 1 — COSO PRINCIPLE REFERENCE (1-2 sentences)
    "COSO Principle N requires [full principle statement]..."
    "The principle's points of focus include [point name where relevant]..."

PART 2 — SCENARIO-SPECIFIC APPLICATION (3-5 sentences)
    Map each fact from the scenario to the principle.
    Use the scenario's own numbers, names, and time periods.

PART 3 — MULTI-FACTOR ANALYSIS (2-4 sentences)
    Show how multiple scenario facts converge on the conclusion.

PART 4 — BUSINESS INTERPRETATION (1-3 sentences)
    "In practice, a controller should ensure that..."

PART 5 — EXAM TRAP / PEDAGOGICAL NOTE (1-2 sentences, recommended)
    "A common area of confusion is between [X] and [Y]..."
```

Minimum: 500 characters.

## 3. ExplanationWrong — Three-Part Mandatory Structure (per distractor)

```
PART 1 — PARTIAL CREDIT
    "Option [X] correctly identifies [partial truth]..."

PART 2 — SPECIFIC ERROR
    "However, [the specific flaw]..."
    "This confuses [concept X] with [concept Y]..."
    "The scenario explicitly states [contradicting fact]..."

PART 3 — CANDIDATE MISCONCEPTION
    "A candidate selecting this option may be [specific cognitive error]..."
```

Minimum: 80 words per slot (100+ words preferred for Analyze/Evaluate).

## 4. COSO Concept Distinctions — Most Tested

| Concept Pair | Distractor Pattern |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Risk Appetite vs. Risk Tolerance | Correctly invokes one, misattributes the other |
| Inherent Risk vs. Residual Risk | Assesses one but should have assessed the other |
| Preventive vs. Detective Control | Identifies the right control type but places it at wrong point |
| Design Deficiency vs. Operating Effectiveness | Diagnoses one when facts describe the other |
| Entity-Level vs. Process-Level | Addresses at wrong scope level |
| Monitoring (P16) vs. Risk Assessment (P7) | Temporal confusion |
| First Line vs. Second Line vs. Third Line | Assigns responsibility to wrong line |
| Ongoing Evaluation vs. Separate Evaluation (P16/P17) | Conflates types |
| Authorization vs. Authentication | Conflates identity with permissions |
| Risk Reduction vs. Risk Sharing vs. Risk Avoidance | Proposes one when ERM requires all four |

## 6. Quality Gates — Six Commitments

1. **Unique scenario** — No two items share a scenario skeleton
2. **Named stakeholders** — Specific decision-makers with roles and documented behavior
3. **Quantified stakes** — Dollar amounts, percentages, time periods, counts (minimum 4)
4. **Cognitive tension** — Competing viewpoints, trade-offs, or diagnostic ambiguity
5. **Choice-specific explanations** — Every EW slot unique, substantive, teaches why that choice is wrong
6. **Framework precision** — COSO Principle numbers, ERM concepts cited exactly
