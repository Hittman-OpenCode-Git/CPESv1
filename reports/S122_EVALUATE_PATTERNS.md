## Pattern E1: Trade-Off Analysis

- **Definition:** Choosing between options where each has identifiable costs and benefits and no option strictly dominates. The candidate quantifies trade-offs, applies decision criteria, and recommends the cost-minimizing *and* strategically appropriate choice.
- **Structure template:**
  1. Present 2-3 alternatives with quantified costs and benefits
  2. Include constraints (capacity limits, seasonality, budget caps)
  3. Add qualitative factors that may override pure cost minimization
  4. Ask: "Which strategy should the manager recommend?"
- **Key differentiator:** Apply items compute the cheaper option. Evaluate items require the candidate to recognize when the *second-cheapest* option is better due to qualitative factors or strategic alignment.
- **Exemplar QIDs:** P1-B-024, P1-BD-021
- **Distractor strategy:**
  - "Choose the cheapest regardless" — ignores quality, reliability, morale
  - "Split evenly" — false compromise capturing benefits of neither
  - "Avoid overtime at all costs" — may be more expensive overall
- **CMA relevance:** Sections D (Cost Management) and B (Planning). IMA emphasizes evaluating alternatives — not just identifying them.

## Pattern E2: Constraint Optimization

- **Definition:** Limited resources (budget, capacity, time) allocated across competing priorities. The candidate determines which combination maximizes objectives under binding constraints, using both quantitative ranking and strategic judgment.
- **Structure template:**
  1. Define the constraint (total budget, capacity limit, timeline)
  2. Present competing demands with associated metrics (NPV, payback, strategic scores)
  3. Include organizational priorities that may override pure financial ranking
  4. Ask: "Which allocation best satisfies the board's criteria?"
- **Exemplar QIDs:** P1-F-048, P1-BD-018
- **Distractor strategy:**
  - "Fund everything proportionally" — dilutes impact
  - "Rank by single metric (NPV only)" — ignores strategic alignment and risk
  - "Fund the largest items first" — bias toward big projects, high-ROI small projects starved
- **CMA relevance:** Core to capital budgeting and resource allocation across Sections B and D.

## Pattern E3: Capital Allocation Decision

- **Definition:** Choosing which capital investments to fund given competing proposals with different risk profiles, strategic implications, and financial returns.
- **Structure template:**
  1. Present 2-3 capital project proposals with NPV, payback, strategic alignment, risk profile
  2. Include organizational context (revenue mix, margin profiles, board priorities)
  3. Include constraints (capital budget cap, mandatory spending, timeline)
  4. Ask: "Which recommendation should the CFO present to the board?"
- **Key differentiator:** Apply items calculate NPV/payback for a single project. Evaluate items present multiple funded projects with genuine trade-offs where financial ranking alone is insufficient.
- **Exemplar QIDs:** P1-BD-014, P1-BD-015, P1-BD-019
- **Distractor strategy:**
  - "Fund the shortest payback" — ignores strategic value
  - "Fund the highest margin potential" — ignores execution risk and timeline
  - "Reject all and return capital to shareholders" — evades the capital allocation decision
  - "Fund a little of each" — half-funding is often worse than not funding
- **CMA relevance:** Directly tests capital budgeting. CMA expects candidates to move from calculation to recommendation.

## Pattern E4: Technology Governance Decision

- **Definition:** Choosing among technology architecture options, system migration strategies, or access control models where each option has different cost, risk, and operational implications.
- **Structure template:**
  1. Present a technology problem (aging ERP, access control incident, data architecture gap)
  2. Offer 3-4 strategies with different cost/time/risk profiles
  3. Include organizational context and business continuity requirements
  4. Ask: "Which strategy should the CTO recommend?"
- **Key differentiator:** Understand items define cloud computing models. Evaluate items require selecting among migration strategies with genuine trade-offs affecting business operations.
- **Exemplar QIDs:** P1-F-030, P1-F-033
- **Distractor strategy:**
  - "Choose the cheapest — technology is always overpriced" — ignores root cause elimination
  - "Choose the most comprehensive regardless of cost" — ignores budget constraints
  - "The existing system is fine — add more manual controls" — anti-pattern
  - "Outsource everything to a vendor" — transfers risk but not governance responsibility
- **CMA relevance:** Section F (Technology). 2024 CMA CSO significantly expanded technology governance content.

## Pattern E5: Variance Investigation Policy

- **Definition:** Designing or evaluating a policy for when to investigate variances. The candidate balances investigation cost against the risk of missing genuine problems, and recognizes when a reported variance is a measurement artifact rather than a performance signal.
- **Structure template:**
  1. Present the current investigation policy with cost and accuracy data
  2. Reveal problems: high false-positive rate, missed trends, standard-setting artifacts
  3. Offer alternative approaches (statistical control charts, risk-based tiered, trend-triggered)
  4. Ask: "Which policy design should the controller recommend?" or "Is this variance genuine or an artifact?"
- **Key differentiator:** Apply items check whether a variance exceeds a threshold. Evaluate items *design the threshold itself* or determine whether an exceedance is *real* or an artifact.
- **Exemplar QIDs:** P1-BD-004, P1-BD-006, P1-BD-005
- **Distractor strategy:**
  - "Investigate everything — no variance is too small" — economically wasteful
  - "Only investigate unfavorable variances" — favorable variances can also indicate problems
  - "Lower the threshold" — addresses under-detection but worsens false-positive rate
  - "Accept management's explanation without verification" — standard-setting artifacts masquerade as performance
- **CMA relevance:** Management by exception and variance investigation are explicitly tested in Section C.

## Pattern E6: Internal Control Design Judgment

- **Definition:** Evaluating whether a specific control design is adequate given risk, cost, regulatory requirements, and organizational constraints. The candidate judges whether compensating controls suffice, whether SOC report scope matches control objectives, or whether governance structures satisfy COSO principles.
- **Structure template:**
  1. Present a control environment with a specific decision or deficiency
  2. Describe management's rationale, compensating controls, or governance structure
  3. Provide the COSO/IIA/AICPA framework standard that applies
  4. Ask: "Was the control design adequate?" or "Did the board satisfy COSO Principle X?"
- **Key differentiator:** Understand items classify control types. Analyze items diagnose which principle was violated. Evaluate items *judge* whether a control design decision was *adequate*, weighing competing legitimate considerations.
- **Exemplar QIDs:** P1-ED-011, P1-ED-026, P1-ED-029, P1-ED-030, P1-ED-037
- **Distractor strategy:**
  - "Management followed the policy, so it's adequate" — procedural compliance ≠ control effectiveness
  - "The board satisfied exchange independence rules" — COSO independence is behavioral, not formal
  - "The compensating control operated for 3 months before failing" — 97% success on a financial control is failure
  - "Small entities can't be expected to have strong controls" — COSO explicitly addresses scalability
  - "Agile teams self-organize — accountability is collective" — diffusion of accountability is the control failure
- **CMA relevance:** Core to Section E. CMA exam increasingly requires judgment about control adequacy rather than simple classification.

## Pattern E7: Budgeting Strategy Selection

- **Definition:** Choosing among budgeting methodologies (top-down, participative, ZBB, ABB, rolling forecast) for a specific organizational context. The candidate evaluates methodologies against organizational size, complexity, cost structure, culture, and strategic needs.
- **Structure template:**
  1. Present the current budgeting approach and its deficiencies (with data)
  2. Describe the organizational context (size, growth, complexity, culture, industry)
  3. Offer 3-4 alternatives with quantified costs and benefits
  4. Ask: "Which methodology should the CFO recommend?" or "Which departments should pilot ZBB?"
- **Key differentiator:** Understand items define ZBB vs. incremental. Apply items calculate a budget. Evaluate items *select the methodology* that best fits organizational context.
- **Exemplar QIDs:** P1-B-001, P1-B-002, P1-B-003, P1-B-008
- **Distractor strategy:**
  - "Keep the current approach — it's efficient and familiar" — ignores demonstrated deficiencies
  - "Implement ZBB everywhere — it's the most rigorous" — ignores cost and disruption
  - "Let department managers choose their own methodology" — eliminates consistency
  - "Outsource budgeting to consultants" — transfers process but not accountability
- **CMA relevance:** Core to Section B. CMA assesses the candidate's ability to recommend appropriate budgeting approaches.

## Pattern E8: Performance Metric Selection

- **Definition:** Selecting KPIs or performance metrics from a larger pool of candidates, applying selection criteria such as strategic alignment, measurability, actionability, and balance. The candidate resists advocacy bias from department heads and recommends a set that best serves organizational objectives.
- **Structure template:**
  1. Define selection criteria (strategic alignment, actionability, balance, measurability)
  2. Present a pool of candidate metrics proposed by multiple stakeholders with self-serving advocacy
  3. Include data about each metric's relevance, data quality, and alignment with strategic objectives
  4. Ask: "Which KPI set should the FP&A manager select for the board dashboard?"
- **Key differentiator:** Understand items define KPIs. Apply items calculate a KPI value. Evaluate items *select which KPIs* to use, weighing strategic alignment and stakeholder interests.
- **Exemplar QIDs:** P1-F-050
- **Distractor strategy:**
  - "Select all financial metrics — that's what the board cares about" — ignores balanced scorecard
  - "Select the metrics with the best data quality" — may exclude strategically important but harder-to-measure areas
  - "Give each department head equal representation" — false fairness
  - "Pick the metrics the CEO personally prefers" — replaces analytical selection with authority bias
- **CMA relevance:** Section C (Performance Management) and Section F (Technology). KPI selection and dashboard design are explicitly tested.
